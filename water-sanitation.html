Water, Sanitation & Hygiene

Four pillars of community health

Access to clean water, functional sanitation and good hygiene practices are foundational to health and development. Our WASH programmes ensure communities have sustainable solutions that prevent disease, improve nutrition and enable thriving.

💧

Water Access Projects

Constructing and rehabilitating water sources — boreholes, hand pumps, piping systems and rainwater harvesting — that serve entire communities year-round.

🚻

Sanitation Facilities

Building improved pit latrines, VIP toilet facilities and community sanitation blocks with maintenance support and community ownership models.

🧼

Hygiene Education

Comprehensive campaigns and training on handwashing, menstrual hygiene, food safety, illness prevention and personal health practices for all ages.

⚙️

Infrastructure Sustainability

Training community water committees and maintenance teams to ensure long-term functionality and independent management of all WASH infrastructure.

Community-wide coverage
70%Disease Drop
 Featured Project

Solar borehole transforms 500 families in Busuma.

In early 2022, New Grace JS installed our first solar-powered borehole in Busuma Village. Within six months of commissioning, diarrheal disease rates among children under five had dropped by 70% — the most dramatic single-project health impact we have ever measured.

The borehole serves 500 families around the clock, requires no fuel, and is maintained by a trained local water committee that collects a small monthly contribution to fund repairs. Ninety percent of our boreholes remain fully functional five years after installation.

Our Process

How a water project comes to life

Every WASH installation follows a rigorous four-step process to ensure community ownership and lasting results.

🔍
01

Community Assessment

We assess water sources, sanitation gaps and community needs through participatory surveys and water quality testing.

📐
02

Design & Planning

Engineers and community leaders co-design solutions, select sites, and form the local water committee before any digging begins.

🔧
03

Installation & Training

Local workers install the infrastructure while committee members receive hands-on training in operation and basic maintenance.

📈
04

Monitor & Support

Field staff conduct quarterly visits, water quality checks and committee audits — supporting communities for years after completion.

 Water Quality

Beyond the tap — hygiene that sticks

Clean water alone is not enough. Behaviour change is the final — and hardest — step in breaking the cycle of waterborne disease.

🧪

Water Quality Testing

Every water point receives quarterly bacteriological and chemical testing. Communities receive plain-language reports on the safety of their water — in their own language.

Quarterly testing cycle
🧼

Handwashing Campaigns

We installed 45 handwashing stations across the district, backed by demonstration sessions for children and caregivers that have measurably changed daily behaviour.

↓ 62% waterborne disease
🌸

Menstrual Hygiene Management

Girls receive hygiene kits, private latrine access and MHM education — keeping them in school every day and eliminating a major driver of female dropout.

90 girls supported
